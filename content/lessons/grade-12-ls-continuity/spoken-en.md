# Continuity — spoken English (classroom)

Hook. Look at this piecewise function. Both sides approach 2, so the limit exists. Many students stop there and write: continuous at 1. That sentence costs a mark.

One idea. Continuity at a is three conditions together, not one. The function must be defined at a. The two-sided limit must exist. And those two numbers must be equal.

Worked example. Left of 1, x plus 1 goes to 2. Right of 1, 2x goes to 2. The two-sided limit is 2. f of 1 is 3, not 2. Not continuous at 1.

Graph. Open circle at height 2, filled point at 3. That visible jump is the missing third condition.

Common mistake. “The limit exists, so it is continuous.” No. Values must match. |x| is continuous at 0 and not differentiable there.
